# SharP: Our Solution

**Category:** AI in Education · **Document 2 of 5:** Proposed Solution

## One home for physics, with a tutor that sees what you see

SharP is a free, community-driven platform that unifies three aspects: interactive physics simulations, an AI tutor that takes part in the learner's experiment, and a community that decides what must be built next for them.

Each answers one of the three absences in the problem statement. A home. A guide. A voice.

## Psi: the guide

Psi is SharP's AI tutor, and it lives inside every simulation.

**It sees what you see.** Psi is not a chatbot placed beside a lesson. It knows which simulation is open, what every control is set to, and what the equations on screen currently read. Ask it a question and it answers about *your* experiment, at *your* settings, not about physics in general.

**It answers from the books.** Psi's explanations are grounded in a curated knowledge base built from standard university physics textbooks and SharP's own teaching material. Its answers rest on the same sources a physics student is taught from, not on a model's memory alone.

**It shows, not only tells.** This is what no textbook and no general AI assistant can do. When an idea is better seen than described, Psi offers the next experiment as a single button: thin the barrier, cool the system, raise the energy. One click, and the simulation moves by itself to show the idea and then Psi explains it too. 

**It cannot break the experiment.** Psi never touches a simulation directly. It can only propose. Every proposal is checked against the simulation's real limits before it is offered, and nothing moves until the learner clicks. Psi proposes; the learner decides.

**It listens.** Questions can be typed or spoken.

**It is there before you choose a simulation.** On the home page, a learner can bring Psi a concept, a derivation or a paradox, and when a simulation fits the question, Psi points them to it.

The result is a different kind of learning moment. The learner asks "why did that happen?" and is no longer alone. Something that can see the same screen answers, and then says: here, watch this.

## The Idea Lab: the voice

On SharP, the learners decide what gets built.

**Propose.** In the Idea Lab, anyone can describe the simulation they wish existed. They choose the world it belongs to, the branch of physics, the simulation they have in mind, the things they want to be able to control, and what they hope to see. And if something or some observable is missing from what options are available, they can add their own, telling their own complete story about what they want to explore.

**Confirm.** A proposal is confirmed through a link sent by email, so every voice on the platform belongs to a real person. Proposing an idea counts as voting for it.

**Vote.** Every proposal enters a weekly community poll. Visitors support the ideas they want most, one confirmed vote per person each week.

**Build.** When the poll closes, the idea with the most support moves onto the public build board, where everyone can see what is being made. A new poll opens at once.

**Deliver.** When the simulation goes live, the person who proposed it is told. Past polls stay on record, so the community can see its own decisions and what came of them.

This is a complete loop, and it is running today: a learner's wish becomes a proposal, a proposal becomes a vote, a vote becomes a simulation. The platform grows in the direction its users choose, not the direction its makers guess.

## Where the two become one idea

Every simulation on SharP is written to one shared convention, and Psi understands any simulation that follows it.

So when a new simulation joins the platform, it does not arrive alone. **It arrives with Psi already attached.** Every addition makes the library larger and Psi more capable in the same stroke.

That is why the community and the AI are not two features of SharP. The community decides what Psi will be able to teach next.

## What comes next

- **Simulations built by the community.** Beyond proposing and voting, contributors will be able to submit simulations of their own. Once reviewed, a contribution will be published under its author's name and understood by Psi from its first day.
- **Experiments the physics has already checked.** Before Psi offers an experiment, the simulation's own physics will confirm that the experiment produces the effect Psi described.
- **A tutor that stays for the result.** After a learner runs an experiment, Psi will return to explain what just changed on screen and why.
- **More of physics.** SharP begins where the need is sharpest: quantum mechanics, statistical physics, solid-state physics and electron transport, because these are fields that will shape neuromorphic computing and superconductivity, which, combined, stand to bring the next technological revolution in the history of humankind. From there, the community's votes decide where it grows.

## Why SharP

Other tools give a learner a simulation, or an explanation, or a community. SharP is built to be the one place that gives a simulation that can explain itself, inside a community that decides what it becomes, at no cost to the learner.

Physics belongs to everyone who has ever looked up and asked why. SharP is where they can ask, and be answered.
