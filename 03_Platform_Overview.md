# SharP: Platform Overview

**Category:** AI in Education · **Document 3 of 5:** How SharP Works for the Person Using It

A visitor arrives as a learner and can leave as someone who helped decide what the platform becomes.

## 1. Arrive: the home page

- **A first impression that is physics itself.** The page opens on a living, moving backdrop and SharP's promise: *physics before and beyond equations*.
- **A live showcase.** Simulations play on the home page, so a visitor sees what the platform does before clicking anything.
- **Psi from the first moment.** A visitor can ask Psi about a concept, a derivation or a paradox without opening a simulation. When a simulation fits the question, Psi offers to take them there.
- **A searchable catalogue.** Every simulation is listed and can be found by keyword, organised into SharP's four starting areas: Quantum Mechanics, Statistical Mechanics, Solid State Physics and Electron Transport.

## 2. Explore: the simulation page

Each simulation opens in its own focused workspace with the experiment on one side and a panel on the other.

- **A simulation you can touch.** The experiment responds as the learner changes it. Many simulations can be handled directly on the canvas, with a short on-screen hint that says how.
- **Controls.** Sliders and switches for the quantities that matter, each labelled with its unit, plus playback, speed and reset.
- **Live mathematics.** The equations on screen carry the current values and update as the learner moves a control, so the formula and the picture stay in step.
- **Theory.** A dedicated tab holds the explanation and equations behind the simulation, written for the state the learner has set up.
- **Psi.** A third tab opens the tutor, right beside the experiment.

## 3. Ask: Psi inside the simulation

- **Ask in your own words,** by typing or by speaking.
- **Psi already knows the context.** It is aware of the open simulation and its current settings, so the learner never has to describe what they are looking at.
- **Answers appear as they are written,** with properly typeset mathematics.
- **Suggested starting questions** are offered for a learner who does not yet know what to ask.
- **Psi remembers the conversation,** so a follow-up such as "why?" builds on what was just said.

## 4. Experiment: one click

- **Suggested actions.** Under an answer, Psi can offer buttons that set up the next thing worth seeing.
- **The simulation moves by itself.** On a click, the controls glide to their new values and the experiment changes in front of the learner.
- **The learner stays in charge.** Nothing changes without a click, and every control remains free to adjust afterwards.
- **Psi can lead to another simulation** when the question belongs somewhere else on the platform.

## 5. Propose: the Idea Lab

When a learner wants something SharP does not have yet, they say so.

- Choose the kind of simulation and the branch of physics.
- Name the simulation and what should be controllable in it.
- Describe what they hope to see.
- Confirm through a link sent to their email.

The proposal joins that week's community poll straight away, and proposing counts as the proposer's vote.

## 6. Vote and follow: the Leaderboard

- **The weekly poll.** Every open proposal, with its support. A visitor can back an idea already on the list; each confirmed email has one vote per weekly poll.
- **The build board.** When a poll closes, the leading idea moves here, where everyone can see what is being built.
- **Past polls.** A record of earlier results, so the community can see what it chose.
- **A message when it is live.** The person who proposed a simulation is emailed when it is published.

## 7. Meet the team

An About page introduces the people behind SharP and what Psi is.

## What makes the experience whole

- **Nothing to install.** SharP runs in a web browser.
- **Free to use.** No account is needed to explore, simulate or ask.
- **One continuous path.** Watching, asking, experimenting, proposing and voting all happen in the same place, so a learner never has to leave to take the next step.
